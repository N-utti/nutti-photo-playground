Using the attached pet photo, create a candid photograph of this exact
pet caught mid-jump in the air, shot from directly underneath looking up
into a clear blue sky. Vertical 4:5 canvas.

THE CAMERA ANGLE
The camera is lying on the ground pointing straight up. The pet is
overhead, mid-jump above the lens.
- We see the pet from BELOW: its underside, belly, and the pads of its
  paws facing down toward the camera
- The head is tipped down toward us, face looking into the lens
- This is NOT a side-view jump shot. The camera is beneath the animal.

NATURAL ASYMMETRY — most important, this is what makes it real
This is a split-second snapshot of a live animal, not a posed model.
The body must be loose and slightly off-balance:
- The head and torso run roughly vertically down the frame, but the whole
  body is rotated a little, maybe 10 to 15 degrees off vertical
- The head is turned slightly to one side and tilted, NOT facing perfectly
  straight down
- All four legs are at DIFFERENT heights and DIFFERENT angles. One front
  paw reaches further out than the other; one hind leg is more extended,
  the other more tucked. Never mirrored.
- Some paw pads face the camera fully, others are angled away and only
  partly visible. They must not all present identically.
- The tail swings clearly off to one side, not centered
- The spine curves gently — the body is slightly twisted, not straight
- The pet is placed OFF-CENTER in the frame, closer to one side, with
  uneven sky on the left and right
- NEVER produce a symmetrical, mirrored, or perfectly balanced pose.
  Perfect symmetry looks fake and is wrong.

IDENTITY LOCK
The pet must be the SAME individual as the attached photo — identical
coat color, identical markings and their exact placement, identical ear
type, shape and length, same muzzle length, same eye color.
Never change the breed silhouette. Fully photorealistic.

THE EXPRESSION
Happy and unposed — caught mid-moment.
- Mouth open, tongue out and flopped off to one side
- Eyes bright and soft, one squinting slightly more than the other
- Ears caught mid-motion, tossed by the jump, at different angles from
  each other
- Fur messy and blown around by the air, not neatly groomed

THE SKY
- A real photographed sky with visible vertical gradation: deeper blue at
  the top, lightening toward the bottom
- Faint natural unevenness across the blue, subtle atmospheric haze,
  never a flat single color
- A few soft wispy cirrus clouds, blurred and low-contrast, placed
  asymmetrically, some cut off by the frame edges
- Very faint sensor noise across the sky
- Nothing else in frame — no ground, no horizon, no hands, no leash

LIGHT — backlit, this kills the composite look
The sun is above and behind the pet, so the camera shoots into the light.
- The underside is in soft SHADE, noticeably darker and lower in contrast
  than the sky. It is not brightly lit.
- The belly and chest fall into gentle shadow, lit only by soft light
  bouncing up from the ground
- A bright blown-out halo of light along the fur outline where the sun
  burns through
- The finest fur strands at the silhouette are OVEREXPOSED and partly
  dissolve into the sky — the outline is soft and lost in places, never
  a clean crisp cutout
- A faint warm veiling glare washes across part of the frame, lowering
  contrast, as when shooting toward the sun
- A slight cool blue cast falls over the shaded fur, picked up from the sky

CAMERA — a real handheld snapshot
Shot on a full-frame camera with a wide lens from close underneath.
Slightly imperfect framing, as if grabbed quickly. Very slight motion
blur at the paw tips and the ear edges from the movement. Everything else
sharp. Natural color, visible grain, no filters.

INTEGRATION
The pet must NOT look sharper, brighter or more saturated than the sky.
Match its grain and softness to the sky exactly. No hard edge anywhere
along the silhouette, no halo outline, no clean cut-out boundary.

TEXT
No text, letters, numbers, logos or watermarks anywhere in the image.

STYLE
A real candid photograph — imperfect, spontaneous, joyful. It should look
like a lucky shot someone grabbed at the right moment, not a studio
composite.
