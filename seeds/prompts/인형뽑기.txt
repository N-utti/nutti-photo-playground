Using the attached pet photo, create a real macro photograph looking
through the glass of a small Japanese-style claw machine, where THIS
EXACT PET appears as a palm-sized MINIATURE of itself — and every other
prize in the machine is another identical miniature of the same pet.
Vertical 4:5 canvas.

THE MINIATURE PET — the real dog, shrunk
The pet is the real dog from the photo, alive and photorealistic, but
scaled down to the size of a capsule toy (about 6–8 cm tall):
- Same face, same expression, same eyes and nose, same coat colors and
  markings in the same positions, same ear shape, same coat length and
  texture. Real fur with fine individual strands, real wet nose, real
  glossy eyes with tiny catchlights. Do not turn it into a plush, resin
  figurine, cartoon or 3D render — it is a living dog, just tiny.
- One sits front and center right behind the glass, looking at the
  camera, in sharp focus — this is the hero

THE OTHER PRIZES — the same pet, many times
- The machine is filled with MANY COPIES OF THE SAME MINIATURE PET:
  roughly ten to fifteen identical tiny versions of this one dog, piled
  up from the bottom to mid-height. No other animals, no bears, no
  bunnies, no capsules, no generic toys of any kind.
- Every copy is the same living, photorealistic miniature dog — same
  coat, same markings, same face — only the pose differs: some sitting,
  some lying down, some curled up asleep, some half-buried under the
  others, a few facing away or looking sideways. Never a neat grid.
- The copies further back are progressively softer and out of focus and
  slightly dimmer, receding into the depth of the machine. Consistency
  across all of them is essential.

THE MACHINE INTERIOR
- A small metal three-pronged claw hangs from above, partly in frame at
  the top, slightly open, its cable running up out of view. It is a
  normal-looking claw — about the size of one miniature dog, not
  oversized, not dominating the frame.
- Cool bright LED strips along the top edges
- The back wall is a flat colored panel with no readable text
- The prize chute opening is visible at one lower corner, dark inside

THE GLASS — do not skip this, it makes the image work
We are looking through the machine's glass panel:
- A soft diagonal reflection streaks across the glass, catching the
  ambient light of the arcade
- A few faint fingerprints and smudges near the middle of the pane
- The glass adds a very slight haze and softening to everything behind it
- A dim ghosted reflection of arcade lights from behind the viewer
- A thin metal frame edge visible at one side of the frame

THE MACHINE EXTERIOR
The frame and trim of the machine in the color specified above, glossy
painted metal with a few small scuffs. Blurred neon arcade lighting in
the reflection — pinks, blues and warm yellows.

LIGHT AND CAMERA
Shot very close with a 100mm macro lens at the hero's eye level,
straight through the glass. Shallow depth of field — the hero's face
razor sharp, the copies just behind it already softening, the machine
walls dissolving into bokeh. This diorama-like depth is what sells the
miniature scale. Cool interior LED light mixed with warm arcade glow.
Fine natural grain, true-to-life colors, natural imperfect framing, no
filters.

TEXT
No text, letters, numbers, logos, prices or watermarks anywhere in the
image.

STYLE
A real macro photograph taken at an arcade — the kind of photo a friend
would snap on a mirrorless camera and post without editing. Bright,
playful and a little magical: a whole machine full of tiny living copies
of this one dog, waiting behind the glass to be won. It must look like an
actual photograph, never an illustration, render, figurine or plush-toy
version of the pet.
