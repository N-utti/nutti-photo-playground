Rebuild the ENTIRE attached photo — the pet and the whole scene around it
— as a highly detailed miniature diorama made of interlocking plastic
construction bricks. Vertical 4:5 canvas.

THE MOST IMPORTANT RULE — THERE IS NO FUR
This sculpture has NO fur, NO hair, and NO soft surfaces of any kind.
It is a hard plastic object. Do not attempt to depict fur texture
anywhere, in any form, at any scale.
The pet's coat is represented ONLY by flat color changes between bricks —
never by shape, never by texture, never by anything protruding.
Wherever the real pet has fluffy or wispy fur (around the muzzle, the
cheeks, the chest, the ear tips, the legs), the sculpture instead has a
completely smooth, hard, flat brick surface with clean straight edges.
Treat the pet as a solid geometric object, like a piece of architecture.

GEOMETRY — everything is right angles
The entire surface of the build consists ONLY of horizontal faces and
vertical faces meeting at 90 degrees.
- No diagonal surfaces. No curves. No rounded edges. No tapered shapes.
- No piece is ever tilted, rotated, angled, or set at anything other
  than flat-on-grid
- Nothing protrudes, spikes, fans out, curls, feathers, or sticks out
- Every silhouette edge is a clean staircase of square steps
- If any part of the image contains a soft, wispy, curved or diagonal
  edge, it is WRONG

RESOLUTION — fine, but still perfectly square
Built from thousands of very small bricks, mostly 1x1 and 1x2 sizes,
packed densely like 3D pixels.
- The head alone contains several hundred small bricks
- Volume and roundness are suggested ONLY by many small square steps,
  like a low-resolution 3D voxel model
- More small pieces is better than fewer large ones — but every single
  one stays axis-aligned and flush with its neighbours
- Think of it as a voxel sculpture: a dog rendered in cubes

COLOR FIDELITY — this is what makes it recognizable
- Reproduce the pet's actual coat colors using many closely-related brick
  shades placed side by side
- Lighter bricks where light falls, darker bricks in shaded areas,
  transitioning in stepped pixel-like dithering
- Markings recreated as blocks of a different brick color in exactly the
  same positions and shapes as the photo
- All shading comes from COLOR only, never from surface shape

THE PET
- Same pose, same proportions, same camera angle as the original photo
- Muzzle length, head shape, body length and stance must match the real
  pet's silhouette closely
- THE MUZZLE AREA: built as a solid rectangular block stepping out from
  the face. Completely smooth and flat. No whiskers, no fringe, no
  scruffy edges. A small black square nose brick and a simple flat mouth.
- THE EARS: each ear is one solid dense mass of small bricks with a

  flat horizontal row of bricks — a clean straight cut, never tapered,
  never wispy, never ragged. Both ears built identically.
- Legs are dense stacked columns with square stepped contours
- Simple round printed eyes

THE BACKGROUND — rebuild it, don't replace it
Recreate the actual environment from the attached photo in bricks,
keeping the same layout, colors and objects:

- Trees become brown brick trunks with densely stacked green leaf pieces

- Objects near the pet are rebuilt as small brick versions in the same

Keep the original composition and camera angle.

PHYSICAL REALISM

- Clean seam lines between individual bricks across the whole build
- Hard glossy injection-molded plastic with sharp specular highlights

- Faint mold lines and small scuffs
- Soft studio lighting from above, crisp shadows in the seams
- Shallow depth of field, the pet's face in sharp focus

STYLE
Photorealistic toy photography of a real physical brick sculpture.
