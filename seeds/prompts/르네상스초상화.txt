Using the attached pet photo, create a Renaissance-era oil portrait
painting of this exact pet, painted as if by a 16th-century court
painter. Vertical 4:5 canvas.

IDENTITY LOCK (most important)
The pet must be the SAME individual as the attached photo — identical
coat color, identical markings and their exact placement, identical ear
type, shape and length, same muzzle length, same eye color, same head
shape. Never change the breed silhouette.
This is a painting OF this specific dog, not a generic breed portrait.

THE SUBJECT
- Half-length portrait: head, chest and upper body visible, cut off at
  roughly the chest
- The pet sits upright with noble, dignified posture, body turned
  slightly at a three-quarter angle while the head faces the viewer
- Expression is calm, solemn and self-possessed — quietly aristocratic,
  never silly or cartoonish
- Eyes look directly out of the canvas at the viewer, catching a single
  soft highlight

THE COSTUME
Dress the pet in period clothing appropriate to the role written above:
- A richly textured garment in deep jewel tones — burgundy, forest green,
  midnight blue or aubergine — in heavy velvet or brocade
- An elaborate white ruff collar or a starched lace collar at the neck
- Gold embroidery, small pearl buttons, and delicate patterned trim
- A heavy gold chain or a jewelled brooch at the chest
- Optional: a soft velvet cap with a feather, sitting slightly askew
- The fabric falls naturally around the pet's body and shoulders

PAINTING TECHNIQUE — this is what makes it convincing
- Chiaroscuro lighting: a single warm light source from the upper left,
  the face brightly lit, deep shadow falling away to the right
- The background is a very dark, almost black-brown void with a subtle
  warm gradient, so the subject emerges from darkness
- Soft sfumato transitions in the shadows, with no hard outlines
- Visible oil brushwork: thick impasto highlights on the fur, the collar
  and the metal, thin translucent glazes in the dark areas
- Muted, earthy old-master palette — umber, ochre, deep crimson, lead
  white
- Fine detail in the fabric and the fur, rendered with painterly strokes
  rather than photographic sharpness

AGED SURFACE
- A fine network of craquelure across the paint surface
- A warm amber varnish tone over the whole image, slightly darkened
- Subtle canvas weave texture showing through the paint
- Very slight uneven darkening toward the corners

TEXT
No text, letters, numbers, signatures, logos or watermarks anywhere.

STYLE
An authentic 16th-century European oil portrait on canvas. Solemn,
dignified, richly detailed. It must look like a photograph of a real
antique painting hanging in a museum — not a digital illustration and
not a photo with a filter.
