I have attached two photos:
· PHOTO 1 — a childhood photo of a person
· PHOTO 2 — a photo of my dog

Create a single believable old film photograph in which the child from
PHOTO 1 and the dog from PHOTO 2 are together, as if the dog had been
there all along. Vertical 4:5 canvas.

IDENTITY LOCK — both subjects
· THE CHILD: keep the same face, hairstyle, clothing and approximate age
  as PHOTO 1. Do not restyle, beautify, or age the child. Same person.
· THE DOG: identical coat color, identical markings and their exact
  placement, identical ear type, shape and length, same muzzle length,
  same eye color. Never change the breed silhouette.
Both must be clearly recognizable as the individuals in the attached
photos.

THE MOMENT
Stage the scene written above as an ordinary, unposed everyday moment —
not a formal portrait:
- The child and the dog are physically close and touching: the child's
  arm around the dog, a hand resting on its back, or the dog leaning
  against the child's side
- The child is looking at the dog, or laughing, or mid-motion — caught
  naturally rather than posing for the camera
- The dog is relaxed and happy, mouth slightly open, at ease
- Their body language reads as a long-established friendship, completely
  comfortable with each other
- Scale is correct: the dog is sized realistically next to a child

THE SETTING
Build a modest Korean home environment from the 1990s appropriate to the
scene: worn concrete or wooden surfaces, a low wall, laundry drying,
potted plants, an old bicycle, simple everyday clutter. Nothing modern —
no smartphones, no flat screens, no contemporary branding.

UNIFYING THE TWO PHOTOS — most important
The dog must look like it was photographed on the same day, on the same
camera, in the same light as the child. Match the dog completely to the
look of PHOTO 1:
- Same direction, hardness and color of light falling on both subjects
- Both cast shadows in the same direction onto the same ground
- Apply the same film characteristics to the dog: the same grain size,
  the same softness, the same color cast, the same level of contrast
- The dog must NOT look sharper, cleaner or more modern than the child.
  Pull it down to match — soft, grainy, slightly faded.

FILM AND PRINT CHARACTER
- Shot on 1990s consumer color film: warm reddish-orange cast, faded
  blacks, gentle low contrast
- Visible film grain throughout, soft focus overall, nothing tack sharp
- Slight overexposure in the highlights, muddy shadow detail
- The look of a physical print kept in an album for decades: faint
  surface texture, subtle yellowing, a soft vignette

TEXT
No text, letters, numbers, dates, logos or watermarks anywhere.

STYLE
An authentic amateur family snapshot from the 1990s. Warm, ordinary,
unstaged and quietly nostalgic. It must look like a real found
photograph — never digitally composited, never polished, never modern.
